# Review-IPM evidence notes

Information Processing & Management (IPM / IP&M, Elsevier, ISSN 0306-4573, e-ISSN 1873-5371).
Collected 2026-10-30, for a "Review-IPM" skill that simulates reviewers before submission.

## 0. How to read these notes

**Tags**
- **[IPM]**: from the journal, its guide, its editor, or its published articles.
- **[ELSEVIER]**: publisher-wide guidance, not specific to IPM.
- **[GENERAL]**: published methods literature, or other journals' editors.
- **[3RD-anecdote]**: third-party or crowd-sourced; small samples, selection bias, often undated.
- **+inferred** added to any tag: my inference from that source type. No source states it as an IPM rule.

**Limits of the evidence**
- ScienceDirect returned 403 to direct fetch. Everything marked "(snippet)" comes from search-engine extracts of the ScienceDirect page; I did not read the full page. Check these against the live page before relying on the exact wording.
- **No public IPM reviewer form, reviewer report or desk-rejection letter was found.** Sections 3 to 5 are therefore a checklist assembled from the guide, Elsevier's generic criteria, editor talks, published IPM papers and anecdotes. They do not describe IPM's actual form.
- Quotes are at most 15 words; everything else is paraphrase.

## 4. Reviewer criteria checklist

**A. From Elsevier's generic guidance** [ELSEVIER] ([How to review](https://www.elsevier.com/reviewer/how-to-review))
1. Title accurately describes content and is findable.
2. Abstract reflects essentials, including major results and limitations.
3. Introduction: current background, clear rationale, objectives stated.
4. Methods detailed enough to replicate; design identified; statistics clearly described. (The page's sample-size and clinical wording is medical; adapt to items, tasks or queries.)
5. Results: appropriate tables and figures; are confidence intervals, effect sizes or sensitivity analyses needed?
6. Conclusions supported by data and design; limitations acknowledged.
7. References current and relevant.
8. Overall: novel or important for this journal and field? Integrity concerns?
9. Data: insufficient data points, unclear tables, and non-significant variation presented as meaningful count as major flaws.
10. Report conduct: brief summary, overall impression covering novelty, impact and contribution, check highlights and graphical abstract, no spelling nitpicks, no self-citation unless scientifically justified, distinguish opinion from evidence-backed comments.

**B. Typical of IPM papers** (no IPM form confirms these)
11. **Information-science contribution:** does the paper say something about information behaviour, retrieval, access, quality or management, beyond a system score? [IPM +inferred] [scope](https://shop.elsevier.com/journals/information-processing-and-management/0306-4573)
12. **Generalisable insight:** would the finding transfer beyond this dataset, model or benchmark? [IPM +inferred]
13. **Theoretical and practical implications:** recent IPM papers carry explicit sections with these names: [LLM scientific extraction](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0306457326005698), [narrative cognition benchmark](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0306457326004164), and [KG/LLM scientometrics](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0306457326002736) (practical implications). [IPM +inferred] (snippets; three papers show a pattern, not a rule)
14. **Annotation reliability reported:** the narrative benchmark paper above includes an annotation-reliability analysis. [IPM +inferred]
15. **Cost reported:** the extraction paper above reports annotation person-hours and GPU-hours. [IPM +inferred]
16. **Human-in-the-loop recommendations where LLM evaluation is biased:** the scientometrics paper above. [IPM +inferred]
17. **Research questions stated and answered.** Common in this literature; I found no IPM requirement. [IPM +inferred, weak]
18. **Baselines:** text-to-SQL papers in IPM compare with baselines on Spider and BIRD using execution accuracy: [EMLC](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0306457325005011), [Multi-pattern retrieval](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0306457324003376), [Agents + LLM](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0306457325000780). So the topic has precedent at IPM, but these are mostly method papers. An audit or measurement paper must make its own case for fit. [IPM +inferred]
19. **Statistical testing:** repeated runs, intervals, effect sizes, paired tests suited to the design, multiplicity control. See section 6.1. [GENERAL]

## 5. Likely reviewer questions (for the simulation)

All are [IPM +inferred] or [GENERAL]: written by me from the sources above, not quoted from any report.

**Editor at the desk**
1. In one sentence, what does this tell information-science readers that they did not know?
2. Which article type is this: research, methods, review or critical application?
3. Is this a benchmark report, or does it give a transferable finding?
4. Can the reviewer identify the authors from a repository, preprint, or self-citation?
5. Do the cover letter and first page make the case for fit with IPM?

**Contribution reviewer**
6. Why does the error or effect matter to users, data stewards or organisations?
7. Which theoretical construct is extended (e.g. relevance, data quality, trust, measurement validity)?
8. Would the conclusions hold for another benchmark, schema domain or model generation?
9. Who changes what practice on reading this? Are the implications traceable to specific results?
10. Is this already known from closest prior work? (Text-to-SQL example: [Wretblad et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.12243v4) on BIRD noise; [Pervasive Annotation Errors, arXiv 2601.08778](https://arxiv.deeppaper.ai/papers/2601.08778v2/similar), listing only, not read.)

**Methods and statistics reviewer**
11. How was the sample chosen? Justified size? Stratified? Weighted to the population it generalises to?
12. Are items independent? Clustered by database or question template? Do intervals respect that?
13. What is the unit of analysis, and is the test paired?
14. Is the claim about effect size, or only significance? Multiple comparisons?
15. Does the gold standard itself have error? How would that error bias the result?
16. Sensitivity: what happens under another metric, prompt, seed, or model version?

**Annotation reviewer**
17. Who are the human annotators, what training and codebook, were they blind to model identity and to each other?
18. Which coefficient, and why? Per class or only overall? Prevalence reported?
19. How were disagreements resolved? Was adjudication done before or after computing agreement?
20. Was the LLM prompt frozen before validation? Was the validation sample unseen during prompt development?
21. Is LLM-human agreement compared with human-human agreement on the same items?
22. Model version, date, temperature, repeat-run stability, paraphrase stability?

**Open-science reviewer**
23. Where is the preregistration, is it time-stamped before data access, and can a reviewer read it anonymously?
24. Are all deviations listed? Are exploratory analyses labelled?
25. Are data and code available during review without exposing identity?
26. Is AI use in the method described in Methods, and AI use in writing in the declaration?

## 6. General guidance for these study types

### 6.1 Evaluation and benchmark studies
- Survey of 107 ML survey papers: failures split into internal validity (e.g. improper baselines, test-set reuse) and external validity. [GENERAL] [Liao et al., NeurIPS 2021](https://datasets-benchmarks-proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2021/hash/757b505cfd34c64c85ca5b5690ee5293-Abstract-round2.html)
- IR evaluation critique: MRR and ERR violate some metric requirements, and MAP assumes unrealistic behaviour. Disputed: Sakai defends averaging RR. Do not treat either side as settled. [GENERAL] [Fuhr](https://unpaywall.org/10.1145%2F3190580.3190586) (record only), [Sakai's reply described in Moffat/Ferrante exchange](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.11735)
- Reporting effect sizes alongside significance tests: Sakai's systematic review of 840 SIGIR/TOIS papers and his 2014 reform piece. Search-summary level; read before citing. [GENERAL] [Urbano et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.11096) compares test error rates empirically.
- Text-to-SQL gold data have errors, and correcting them can change model rankings: [Wretblad et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.12243v4). The ranking-change claim comes from a secondary summary; confirm in the paper's tables. [GENERAL]

### 6.2 LLM annotation with human agreement
- Validate any LLM annotation against high-quality human labels. In tests with GPT-4 on 27 tasks across 11 datasets, performance varied by dataset and task, and nine tasks had precision or recall below 0.5. [GENERAL] [Pangakis et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.00176v1)
- Test stability by rerunning and paraphrasing prompts; fix and document model versions, because proprietary models change; compare with human inter-coder reliability; validate after the prompt is final. [GENERAL] [Törnberg 2024, *Sociologica*](https://doaj.org/article/3ec44d709af14213aa1128732441e612), [arXiv](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2402.05129) (some points via secondary summaries)
- Prompt wording itself contributes measurement variance. Preprint. [GENERAL] [arXiv 2604.16413](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.16413)

### 6.3 Kappa interpretation

| Cohen's κ | Common bands (attributed to Landis & Koch 1977; McHugh attributes to Cohen) | McHugh 2012 |
|---|---|---|
| ≤ 0.20 | Slight | None (0 to 0.20) |
| 0.21 to 0.40 | Fair | Minimal (0.21 to 0.39) |
| 0.41 to 0.60 | Moderate | Weak (0.40 to 0.59) |
| 0.61 to 0.80 | Substantial | Moderate (0.60 to 0.79) |
| 0.81 to 1.00 | Almost perfect | Strong (0.80 to 0.90), almost perfect (> 0.90) |

- McHugh treats κ below 0.60 as inadequate and argues the common labels are too lenient for health research. She advises computing both percent agreement and kappa. Her context is health research, so her bands are not a universal rule. [GENERAL] [McHugh 2012](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC3900052/fullTextXML)
- Landis and Koch's bands rest on opinion rather than evidence; κ also depends on the number of categories and subjects. [GENERAL] [Wikipedia: Fleiss' kappa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fleiss%27_kappa) (tertiary)
- Prevalence paradox: high raw agreement can yield low κ under imbalanced marginals; the second paradox concerns symmetry of imbalance. Report raw agreement and marginals; Gwet's AC1 is a suggested alternative, itself debated. [GENERAL] [Feinstein & Cicchetti 1990, record](https://scholarhub.vn/publication/:slug/99d46fd7-7674-4728-9c1a-4edb5f5f85c5), [Cohen vs AC1 comparison](https://www.springermedizin.de/a-comparison-of-cohen-s-kappa-and-gwet-s-ac1-when-calculating-in/9433204)
- **Coefficient choice matters.** Fleiss' kappa extends Scott's pi, not Cohen's kappa, and it assumes randomly sampled raters per item. If the same fixed raters code every item, the same source points to Conger's kappa instead. A reviewer may catch this. [GENERAL] [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fleiss%27_kappa) (tertiary; confirm against Fleiss 1971 or Gwet before citing)
- Chance-agreement choice can move a value across the 0.67 convention, so Artstein and Poesio recommend reporting both versions. [GENERAL] [Artstein & Poesio 2008](https://www.cl.cam.ac.uk/teaching/1516/R216/Artstein.pdf) (snippet; PDF would not parse here)
- Krippendorff's alpha: 0.667 appears as a common minimum rule of thumb. The 0.800 companion threshold is widely repeated, but I could not verify it from a source here; check *Content Analysis* first. [GENERAL] [Krippendorff's alpha guide](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2008.00977)
- Checklist derived from the above (+inferred): name coefficient and why; report CI; report per-class values and prevalence; report raw agreement; report human-human and LLM-human on the same items; do not call κ "substantial" without noting the scale used.

### 6.4 Preregistration claims
- Of 27 preregistered studies in *Psychological Science* (2015 to 2017), two had no deviations and nine disclosed none of theirs; deviations concerned sample size, exclusions and analysis. Deviations can be legitimate, but they must be transparent. [GENERAL] [Claesen et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8548785)
- Reporting template: [Willroth & Atherton 2024, "Best Laid Plans"](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/25152459231213802) define a deviation as any discrepancy between plan and action, and supply a disclosure table (table fields not read). [GENERAL] Summary: [APS Observer](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/observer/methods-preregistration-deviations)
- When to deviate: [Lakens 2024, *Collabra*](https://research.tue.nl/en/publications/when-and-how-to-deviate-from-a-preregistration/). [GENERAL]
- For the simulation (+inferred): reviewer opens the registry, checks the timestamp against data access, compares hypotheses, sample, exclusions and analysis one by one against the paper, and flags "preregistered" used in the abstract for anything exploratory. Also: does the registry link reveal author names under double anonymization?
- I found no IPM statement on preregistration, either requiring or valuing it. [IPM: none found]

### 6.5 AI-use disclosure
- **Authors:** declare AI use in preparation in a separate statement naming tool and purpose and confirming review; basic spelling and grammar checks need no declaration, substantive changes do. **AI used in research design, data collection, analysis or interpretation belongs in Methods, described reproducibly.** So LLM annotation is a method, and it is not covered by the declaration alone. AI-created or edited images need caption disclosure; AI may not fabricate or alter primary data images. [ELSEVIER] [Elsevier Connect, 2026-08-18](https://www.elsevier.com/connect/updated-generative-ai-policies-for-journals-supporting-responsible-use-while-protecting-trust). Same rule in the IPM guide (snippet). [IPM]
- **Reviewers: Elsevier's two pages conflict.**
  - [GenAI review-process policy](https://www.elsevier.com/about/policies-and-standards/the-use-of-generative-ai-and-ai-assisted-technologies-in-the-review-process), last updated July 2026: no upload of the manuscript or reports to AI tools, even only to improve language and readability; no AI in evaluation.
  - [How to review](https://www.elsevier.com/reviewer/how-to-review), undated: no upload of the manuscript, but private tools may support language, structure or background literature search, with disclosure.
  - Use the stricter, dated policy for any real review. The Review-IPM skill's self-review of the author's own manuscript is not reviewing in this sense. [ELSEVIER]
- The IPM guide snippet also says reviewers and editors must not upload unpublished manuscripts to generative AI. [IPM]
- A third-party anecdote in section 7 alleges AI-written IPM reviews. Unverifiable. [3RD-anecdote]
