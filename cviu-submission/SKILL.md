A **2026-11-07** refresh verified the official CAS ZIP and Elsevier LaTeX, AI, data, and review guidance; the journal guide itself returned 403. ## Work within the requested stage

Infer the authorized stage from the conversation: plan only, manuscript revision, evidence development, packaging, or journal-system action. When a user requests approval of a plan before editing, make the proposed changes concrete and reviewable, but leave manuscript and experiments unchanged. Once approved, execute the authorized changes without asking again for the same permission. Reuse confirmed author facts; ask only for a genuinely missing fact that cannot be established from supplied material.

A revision can change experiments as well as prose. Where the research claim needs it, propose or perform authorized additions, supplementary measurements, fair replacements, merges, movement to supplementary material, or removal of redundant presentations. Explain the purpose, minimum evidence, and completion condition. Preserve contradictory material findings and the record of superseded experiments; do not select the reported evidence by which outcome is favorable. Do not expand the study merely to satisfy a generic checklist. See `review-cviu` for an optional evidence-to-action review workflow.

## Preparation workflow

1. Identify the current manuscript, claim, task, prior editorial status, and assets. Distinguish a rejection, ended review, and pending major revision using actual records.
2. Maintain a short matrix of **journal requirement**, **publisher guidance**, **published-example observation**, and **author/editorial recommendation**. Record URL, date, access method, and uncertainty. A publisher's general advice does not override a specific journal instruction.
3. Connect each material gap to a concrete action: text correction, code/configuration check, recalculation, experiment, or author fact. State the smallest evidence needed to close it. Confident phrasing must remain supported by the actual experiment; consolidate repeated caveats without suppressing decisive facts.
4. Prepare editable manuscript, bibliography, figures, tables, highlights, required declarations, and relevant supplements. Preserve publisher template bytes and notices; keep any local compatibility patch separate and documented. Use the journal-linked template, not a commercial imitation.
5. Check agreement among abstract, main text, supplements, highlights, cover letter, and declarations. Render the actual final PDF and inspect all pages, figure labels, equations, citations, tables, and the author list. Validate the source bundle in a clean directory, with no absolute asset paths or missing dependencies. A revised table/figure must retain the source, calculation version, and complete comparison scope.
6. Reconcile files with the actual authorized submission workflow. Keep these states distinct: **files complete**, **locally rebuilt**, **platform-compatible**, **uploaded**, and **submitted**. Do not claim a logged-in workflow was tested from a public landing page. Resolve real system requirements and inspect its generated proof before describing that platform step as complete.

## CVIU-specific preparation anchors

The historical full guide supports single-anonymized review, editable source, a 250-word abstract maximum, 1–7 keywords, separate editable highlights with 3–5 bullets of at most 85 characters each, author–year target references, required declarations/CRediT, data Option C, and requested short author biographies/photos. Consult the requirements reference for scope and dates. No explicit main-text page/word cap was found in that snapshot; do not invent one or convert a typical published length into a rule.

Graphical abstracts are encouraged, not established mandatory. A cover letter can help explain journal fit; do not invent a compulsory length. Keep data-sharing statements factual: a local archive, private Git backup, review attachment, and deposited public research object are different access states.

For a code supplement, distinguish the preserved experiment source from any portable adapter. When reproducing a numerical checkpoint transition, preserve the implemented floating-point operation order; algebraically equivalent expressions can round differently. Keep source/configuration identities with the reported results. Label independent initialization seeds separately from same-seed numerical replays, and retain full-view evidence when providing a deterministic foreground crop. For exact recomputation, record the interpreter and numerical-library versions as well as the source and configuration. Preserve per-observation scalar records, pairing identifiers, and aggregation code alongside tables. A portable reader may map original paths through a hash-bound manifest while leaving the scientific calculation and original records unchanged. These are implementation and reporting recommendations, not additional CVIU submission mandates.

For **Editorial Manager source processing**, the current publisher LaTeX FAQ says to place resources at one folder level. If that is the actual target route, prepare a collision-free flat variant, rewrite references, and compare its rebuilt PDF with the editable master. Preserve the organized master. The FAQ is about source submission, not only production; it gives no exception allowing nested folders merely because they are inside a ZIP. Do not infer current CVIU portal behavior without observing it.

Apply current AI rules to the actual use: manuscript assistance, explanatory diagrams, data-derived plots, primary research images, and graphical abstracts have different conditions. Never fabricate research evidence or disguise generative retouching as an experimental output.

Use DataHub when a local policy designates it for reusable sources. Keep credentials, unpublished manuscript details, personal author facts, raw data, and private paths out of this reusable public skill. The skill itself does not authorize journal submission, public release, contact with editors, or authorship changes.
