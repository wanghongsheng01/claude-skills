# Ten published-paper learning cases

Read on 2026-11-09. These are purposively selected examples, not an acceptance study. Performance numbers below are paper-reported, not reproduced. Eight readings used journal versions; the two author manuscripts are explicitly identified. Use the current journal version before adopting an exact experimental specification.

| Paper and publication | Scientific problem and key operation | Evidence to learn from | Boundary to preserve |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Back-to-Bones](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.patcog.2024.110762), PR156 (2024) | Domain generalization: define a backbone as architecture, pretraining data and training recipe; cross backbone choice with DG algorithms. | §2–3, Tables1/4 separate backbone gains from algorithm increments. Source-domain validation and declared search budgets make comparison assessable. | Correlations with pretraining accuracy do not establish a causal mechanism. Different pretraining data remain different information resources. |
| [CIFD](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.patcog.2024.110338), PR150 (2024) | Single-source generalization: extend local causal-variable discovery, estimate effects, select variables and predict. | §5.4 and Table3 vary shift strength using known synthetic variables. An ablation can help at one shift and hurt at another. | Predictive stability does not alone verify causal identification; graph assumptions and adjustment-set recovery matter. |
| [SAMOSA](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.patcog.2023.109909), PR145 (2024) | Limited supervision: constrain asymmetric semantic/nonsemantic information paths and gradient flow before mixing content. | Fig.3 describes architecture; Fig.4 describes which losses update which components. Table2 separates regularization from added synthetic samples. | For one MT setting, most gain comes from regularization. Equal epochs do not equal equal computation. Its 2023-08 online date differs from its 2024 issue date. |
| [SigTree](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.patcog.2025.111364), PR162 (2025) | Interpretable categorical clustering: choose rule-tree splits using association tests and a multiplicity-adjusted stopping rule. | Tables2/5/6 jointly show clustering quality, tree complexity and a restricted size-matched comparison. | Purity and F-score can disagree. Tree depth is an explanation-complexity proxy, not evidence of human trust or a guarantee of global error control. |
| [Interpretable multi-view clustering](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.patcog.2025.111418), PR162 (2025) | Alternate between shared latent clustering and an original-feature decision tree; let tree outputs constrain representations. | [Read author v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.02644v1), Tables2/3 and depth plots: representation quality and readable rules have a measurable tradeoff. | This 12-page author manuscript has a TKDE placeholder header. It is not the PR typesetting version; exact final tables require comparison with the VoR. Known class count is additional task information. |
| [Big-Clust](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.patcog.2025.111654), PR165 (2025) | Large-scale clustering: retain limited-memory solver information across random batches and select incremental-cluster initializations. | Algorithms1/3/4, Remarks1/7 and §6–7 connect optimization choices with quality, time and data scale. | The stochastic procedure is described as heuristic; good objective values do not prove stationarity or global optimality. Mixed implementation languages and threads constrain timing claims. |
| [Explainable time-series anomaly detection](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.patcog.2024.110826), PR156 (2024) | Preserve frequency meaning in discrete latent tokens; use conditional likelihood for detection and masked resampling for explanation. | [Read author v5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.12550v5), Table2 and §6 connect stride cost and aggregation failures; aligned plots show signal, scores and conditional completion. | A top-k location metric with tolerance is not streaming event F1. Manually measured periods are a resource. Conditional completion is not a causal counterfactual. |
| [Switching Dual-Student](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.patcog.2026.113115), PR175 (2026) | Limited-label segmentation: choose the student supplying a teacher update and modulate its EMA weight by loss. | Tables4/6 separate selection and cumulative modules; Table7 exposes training time and fold variability. | §3.4 text and printed weight formulas have direction inconsistencies; code is not established by the PDF. Fold variation is not training-seed variation, and the method is not lowest-variance in every Table7 comparison. |
| [Dynamic unified bipartite graph](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.patcog.2024.110715), PR156 (2024) | Multi-view clustering: let the unified bipartite graph update the graph that controls filtering; include higher-order view structure. | Eq.8 and Fig.5 distinguish dynamic feedback, static filtering, no filtering and removal of higher-order structure. Tables3–5 retain datasets where competitors win. | An n×n graph remains; §3.4 and §5 acknowledge quadratic sample cost. Anchor graphs alone do not establish end-to-end linear scalability. Selection criteria and repeated-run uncertainty require clarification. |
| [Image-free hierarchical prompt tuning](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.patcog.2025.112986), PR174 (2026) | Cross-modal adaptation: use LLM-generated labeled text to train shared and class-specific prompts with frozen CLIP; transfer prompts to image inference. | Figs.3–4 expose generation settings and parameter sharing; Table3 is a complete 2×2 local/order ablation; Table6 separates supervision resources. | Image-free refers to target adaptation, not CLIP pretraining. Generated text, category hierarchy, validation feedback and cost are resources. Five training seeds do not establish regeneration robustness; Table7 leaves the test direction unspecified. |

## What these examples change in practice

Scientific problems can be studied through controlled measurement as well as new algorithms. Define the comparison unit before attributing gains. Give each mechanism its own observable test, and give every claimed benefit its matching metric. Separate source/target visibility, label access, pretraining and compute. Read algorithm boxes against adjacent prose and equations. Preserve favorable and unfavorable regimes.

These observations are learning examples. They do not create universal requirements for the number of datasets, seeds, tables, citations or modules, and do not establish why an editor accepted a paper.
